Give people and Pokémon on your map a look of their own: a ranger in your town's colors, a gym leader who looks like your friend, or a brand new Pokémon.

## Adding a sprite

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the upload window

Select someone on the **People** layer:

- **Person**: press the **+** tile at the start of **Looks**.
- **Pokémon**: press the **+** tile at the end of **Look**.

You can also add sprites from **City settings → Sprites for people and Pokémon**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Choose the image

Pick a PNG, WebP or GIF with a transparent background. The window previews what the game will use: for a person, each direction walking in place, so a wrong row shows up right away. It also checks the size.

</Step>
<Step>

### Name it and add it

The sprite is selected for the person or Pokémon you were editing, and shows up in their picker for everyone else on the map.

</Step>
</Steps>

Sprites belong to the city, like tilesets: interiors, live rooms, share links and forks all carry them. Logged in, an image can be up to 1 MB. Logged out, it's stored inside the city, up to 256 KB.

## People: a walking sheet

A person needs every direction and a walking step, all in one image.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Grid | 3 columns × 4 rows, every frame the same size |
| Frame size | **16×32 px**, the size of the game's own characters (the whole image is **48×128 px**) |
| Bigger frames | 32×32, 32×48 or 32×64 work too, and are scaled to one tile wide |
| Rows, top to bottom | facing **down**, **up**, **left**, **right** |
| Columns, left to right | **standing**, **left foot forward**, **right foot forward** |
| Background | transparent, with the feet at the bottom of each frame |

```
          standing   left foot   right foot
down    [  16×32  ][   16×32   ][   16×32   ]
up      [         ][           ][           ]
left    [         ][           ][           ]
right   [         ][           ][           ]
```

### RPG Maker sheets

Many character sheets online use RPG Maker layouts. Pick the matching **Layout** in the upload window:

| Layout | Grid | Rows | Frames used |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DreamMist | 3 × 4 | down, up, left, right | standing, step, step |
| RPG Maker VX / MV | 3 × 4 | down, left, right, up | middle column stands |
| RPG Maker XP | 4 × 4 | down, left, right, up | first column stands |

A sheet with 4 columns is detected as RPG Maker XP. VX/MV and DreamMist sheets look the same from the outside, so choose VX/MV by hand when the rows are down, left, right, up.

## Pokémon: one image

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Image | the whole Pokémon in one picture |
| Facing | **left**, in a front 3/4 view (it's flipped when it walks right, and in your party) |
| Size | **64×64 px** is ideal, from 32×32 up to 96×96, at most 256×256 |
| Background | transparent, with the feet near the bottom |

The image is used on the map, at about two tiles tall, and in battle. The **species** you pick still decides its types, stats, moves and catch rate, so choose one that matches your creature. If you give it a name of its own, like *Flamefox*, battles use that name and the player keeps it as a nickname after catching it.

## Making sprites with AI

Image generators can draw a good starting point. Paste one of these prompts, replace the part in brackets, and clean the result up as described below. The same prompts are in the upload window under **Make it with AI**, with a copy button.

Pixel art character sprite sheet for a top-down Pokémon game, in the style of Pokémon Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald (Game Boy Advance).
Character: [describe your character, for example a young ranger with a green cap, a brown vest and a backpack].
Layout: a grid of 3 columns and 4 rows where every cell is exactly the same size, 16×32 pixels per cell (the whole image is 48×128 pixels).

Columns from left to right: standing still, left foot forward, right foot forward.

### Cleaning up an AI image

AI tools rarely hit exact pixel sizes, and often add a background or blurry edges. A few minutes in a pixel editor fixes it:

1. **Shrink it to the exact size** with *nearest neighbor* scaling, so pixels stay sharp. In GIMP: *Image → Scale Image*, interpolation **None**. Aseprite, Piskel and LibreSprite have the same option.
2. **Remove the background** if it isn't transparent. Most editors have a magic wand or *color to alpha*.
3. **Line up the frames.** For a person, every frame should sit in its cell with the feet on the bottom row of pixels. Generating each direction separately and pasting them into a 48×128 grid is often easier than asking for the whole sheet at once.
4. **Check the walk.** Upload it: the preview walks each direction in place, so frames in the wrong order stand out.

<Callout title="Tip">
  Ask the generator for something 8 times bigger, like 384×1024 for a person or 512×512 for a Pokémon, then shrink it down. Big pixel art scales down cleanly; asking for a tiny image usually gives a blurry one.
</Callout>

## Removing a sprite

Open **City settings** and press the trash button next to it. Everyone using it goes back to a default look.

Only upload art you made or are allowed to use, and credit the artists in your city's description on Explore.
